# Filtered Blend — store policy drafts

Filtered Blend is operated by **Farvision LLC** but sells physical goods to
consumers through Shopify and Amazon. That makes it legally different from the
software products:

- It bills through **Shopify Payments**, not the Farvision Stripe account, so
  it has a different merchant account, descriptor, and dispute process.
- Selling goods brings in shipping, title and risk of loss, returns, and food
  labeling — none of which the software Terms cover.
- Buyers are consumers, not business subscribers, so consumer-protection rules
  and UCC implied warranties apply differently.

**Therefore:** the company Terms of Service at `farvisionllc.com/terms`
explicitly do **not** cover Filtered Blend. The company Privacy Policy at
`farvisionllc.com/privacy` **does** — same entity, same controller — so the
store's privacy policy should point at it rather than duplicate it.

## Where these go

Shopify admin → **Settings → Policies**. Shopify links these automatically at
checkout and in the storefront footer. Paste each draft into its matching
field.

### A note on arbitration for the store

The company Terms now require binding individual arbitration with a class
waiver and a 30-day opt-out. **Do not copy that clause into the store terms
without asking counsel specifically about consumer goods.** The calculus is
different:

- Software customers are largely businesses; coffee buyers are consumers, and
  consumer arbitration clauses face more unconscionability scrutiny in
  California.
- Under AAA Consumer Arbitration Rules the business pays most of the fees, so
  for $50 orders arbitration can cost far more than the dispute.
- Amazon-channel orders already fall under Amazon's own dispute process, so
  the clause would only reach direct Shopify sales.

For a store this size, relying on small claims court and California law may
be the better answer than arbitration. That is a judgment call, not a
drafting one.

## Before publishing — verify

- [ ] **Change the statement descriptor.** It currently reads `FARVISION`,
      but customers buy from a brand called Filtered Blend and have no reason
      to recognize Farvision on a card statement. An unrecognized descriptor
      is the leading cause of "I don't recognize this charge" chargebacks, and
      those cost a fee even when you win. Change it in Shopify admin →
      Settings → Payments → Shopify Payments → Manage → Customer billing
      statement to something containing the brand, e.g. `FILTEREDBLEND` or
      `FILTEREDBLEND.COM`. Update the store terms above once changed.
      Until then, the terms disclose the mismatch, and order confirmation
      emails should say what the charge will look like.
- [ ] **FTC Mail Order Rule** — you must ship within the time you promise, or
      within 30 days if you don't state one, and notify the customer with an
      option to cancel if you can't. The processing-time claim above needs to
      be one you actually hit.
- [ ] **Food labeling** — coffee is a food product. Confirm net weight,
      ingredient and roaster identity statements, and any facility or allergen
      disclosures are on the packaging.
- [ ] **Sales tax nexus** — physical goods create nexus differently than SaaS.
      Confirm where you're registered and that Shopify tax settings match.
- [ ] **Amazon seller side** — selling on Amazon puts you under the Business
      Solutions Agreement, which is a separate relationship from SellAvant's
      SP-API developer obligations. Don't let the two blur.
- [ ] **Return window consistency** — the window in the Shopify policy field,
      the storefront copy, and the Amazon listing settings should all agree.
